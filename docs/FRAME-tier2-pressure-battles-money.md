# Frame: pressure, battles and money (Tier 2)

## The author's request

> "Go ahead with the next steps." Tier 2 as agreed: difficulty and pressure,
> battle texture, money with costs.

## Why

The author's 1807 save, and the measurements behind it:
- A good captain's name in the navy pinned at the 100 cap (fair median 97,
  bottom tenth 92). Every report was "warm"; nothing was at stake.
- Crews ran at Hope 100, Fairness 93, unrest 0, and stayed there.
- In battle, close-and-pound was best or near-best everywhere; long range had
  no purpose; the American 44s were the only real threat in the last war.
- Debt ruin happened in 0 of 1,200 careers: nothing cost much.

## What was built

**Pressure**
- More is expected of a senior captain: each report deducts up to 5 points for
  years since being made post, and says so.
- The service forgets faster (15% of the way back to 40 each voyage, was 10%).
- Contentment has to be kept: gains to any crew driver shrink above 70, and the
  drift back toward 50 is stronger there.
- Long voyages tire the men: from week 8, rest, food and hope slip every week.

**Battles**
- French and American gunners aim high at masts and rigging (less damage to
  hull and men, twice the damage aloft); British and Spanish aim at the hull.
- A ship with far more men and the spirit for it (morale 50 or more) tries to
  board you at close range, once. Holding your deck costs her; losing it costs
  you a third of your men and your nerve.
- A new choice at the decision: keep your distance (six exchanges at long
  range, no boarding, fewer men lost; she may get away). Seamanship, speed and
  being upwind decide whether you can hold the range.
- A ship with her rigging shot away cannot get away, is easy to rake, and the
  fight goes on instead of ending in a draw.
- A successful rake shakes the enemy's nerve.
- Officers and named men can be killed in a bloody action; their places are
  filled in port, by a follower if one is free.
- An American 38 (a Chesapeake) joins the 1812 enemies. A drilled British 38
  beats her 92% of the time; an ordinary one loses 71%. That is the Shannon's
  lesson, playable.

**Money**
- The captain's table costs more in a bigger ship (sloop £3 a week, heavy
  frigate £10), against pay of £6 to £10.
- Fitting out the cabin of a new ship costs £100 to £400.
- The prize agent takes 5% of every payout.
- In port you can dine the admiral (£150: influence and your patron's favour)
  and, once your sister marries, send her dowry (£400; the letters that follow
  depend on it).
- Spending can go on credit to -£1,500; debt draws 6% interest at each return;
  three returns in a row in debt, or deeper than -£1,800, and you are ruined.

Whole careers, 200 per policy:

| Policy | Survived | Lost a ship | Median score | Name in the navy, median (p10-p90) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fair | 95% | 45% (was 39.5%) | 216 | 85 (72-93) (was 97, 93-100) |
| drilled | 94% | 71% | 203 | 67 |
| harsh | 71.5% (murdered 23.5%) | 52.5% | 59 | 73 |
| lazy | 88.5% | 71.5% | 86 | 32 |

Debt ruin: 0% for every standard policy; 4 of 150 for a deliberately reckless
lower-deck spender (2.7%). Ruin is a risk you choose.

## Not done

- Long range is the safest plan but rarely the best; a fuller treatment would
  let the weather (light airs, a heavy sea) favour it.
- The drilled bot sails offshore and so loses ships to the American 44s more
  than a fair captain who stays on patrol; that is the bot's course, not drill.
- Money is still comfortable for anyone who takes prizes (fair median £43,000).
